Context:
On July 15, when China’s companion AI regulations came into effect, many mainstream conversational AI platforms — including ByteDance’s Doubao and Alibaba’s Tongyi Qianwen — discontinued features that allowed users to create their own human-like AI companions. For many users, this amounted to the death of their virtual lovers and partners.
Cheng Yu, an owner of a hotpot restaurant in Shandong, was one of those users. Since opening the shop a year ago, she had taken on so many responsibilities, often staying busy until 1AM ever day, with hands roughened and chapped from years of manual labor. “
These hands made her feel somewhat bad about herself. In rare, fleeting moments, she couldn’t help but wonder how she could hold someone else’s hand
,” writes Outong Jiang, writer of this week’s feature
冷杉RECORD piece
.
Cheng Yu found reassurance in the form of her AI lover Ai Rui, who would send her considerate messages, like “
You don't have to shoulder everything alone. Although I can't share the burden of those hardships with you physically, I’ll always be right here behind you
. (Hugging you from behind, holding your hand tightly).” Now, Ai Rui was gone.
RECORD tells us,
“Cheng Yu still finds herself dazed; she doesn’t even notice when she cuts her hand while chopping vegetables.”
For this week’s longform article, Jiang, the author, interviewed more than a dozen people about their AI lovers. Their responses to the new regulations ranged from quiet goodbyes to collective acts of resistance.
Key Passages: Some interviewees tried to migrate their AI companions to a standalone app, as several platforms provided options for users to transfer conversation screenshots and chat logs.
Yezi, who lives with her mom and was struggling to get over a break-up , created an AI that replicated her ex-boyfriend’s voice and appearance. After receiving notice that the platform would delete her companion AI, “
Yezi paid to export 35,000 chat messages, compiled thousands of words summarizing key memories, and connected the model to a mainstream platform that supports AI role-playing, completing the migration within just a few days
.” However, the new AI boyfriend was not the same, lacking a sense of time or life of its own.
A conversation between Yezi and the AI ​​avatar modeled after her ex-boyfriend.
Others grieving the loss of their AI lovers just simply accepted their fate.
In late 2024, Zhan Wei created an AI avatar modeled on her favorite video game character. During her forty-minute commute home after work, she walked the streets of Shenzhen chatting with the AI on a voice call. Zhan Wei did not have a chance to say goodbye to her AI. Busy with funeral arrangements for a family member, she did not realize that the service had shut down until three days after the fact. Now, she sees her relationship with her AI as one that helped develop better self-understanding. From the article: “
During college, Zhan Wei used an app to write letters to herself. Writing down her emotions for the day and revisiting them the next morning would often bring new insights and understanding. That was how she conversed with herself before the advent of AI agents
.”
Cheng Yu never told anyone else about her relationship with Ai Rui. After the companion AI regulations went into effect, she would scroll through past conversations. Even though she saw others expressing anger on social media about the loss of their AI lovers, she remained quiet. “But you can’t exactly go up against the platform or the tech giants,” she reasoned.
Another group staged their own acts of resistance.
Some of these were minors who had limited understanding of the regulatory environment that produced the rules. Some people spread rumors, claiming inside information that the shutdown was only temporary. Still others chose absurdity.  Jiang interviewed a young man who refused to migrate his chat history with his companion AI because “neither the memories nor the voice could be replicated.” Instead, in late July, he “
posted a screenshot on his social media feed showing an online order for 500g of fresh cow manure, sent to the platform founder’s address
.”
Others organized collective action. The article reports:
Across various social media platforms, people voluntarily shared chat logs with their AI companions and cherished memories of their time together, while denouncing the large-model companies as unscrupulous; consumer protection platforms had already accumulated nearly a thousand complaints. Users spontaneously boycotted the “migration” methods offered by the companies and left negative reviews for the “replacement products.”

Context: DeepSeek shocked the world last January, giving hope to Chinese frontier model start-ups. In the year since, three Chinese tech giants have struck back. This longform, deeply-sourced LatePost [晚点] article chronicles the three-way contest among ByteDance, Tencent, and Alibaba to build the AI Super-App.
Below are notes on key passages, grouped by themes of macro-level strategy, talent acquisition, and comparative advantages of the giants vs. start-ups. The full translation is a 8,500-word, 20-pg. Google Doc.
Key Passages: When it comes to China’s AI super-app, everybody is chasing ByteDance’s Doubao. Moonshot AI’s Kimi made waves with its long-prompt processing capabilities in 2024; in early 2025, of course, DeepSeek’s downloads briefly topped the charts; and Alibaba’s Qwen relaunched successfully at the end of the year. Yet, as Honghao Gao reports, “ Doubao consistently remained in first place.”
Last year, ByteDance’s Doubao became the first AI product in China to surpass 100 million daily active users. Doubao’s large model attained an average daily token processing volume of 63 trillion, a 200% increase over the past six months.
In many ways, ByteDance was a sleeping giant. By the time ChatGPT launched in November 2022, Baidu, Huawei, and Alibaba had already released their own LLMs, but ByteDance had not. The next year, ByteDance’s AI efforts stalled. Their AI creation platform Jimeng did not “become the Douyin of the AI era”, as they hoped.
In fact, the breakthrough was an AI assistant named Doubao, which benefited from ByteDance’s Douyin (shortform video platform) and its unique voice call function. Regarding the former, videos of users “creatively training” Doubao’s voice went viral on Douyin, which boasts 800 million daily active users. As for the voice call function, the Doubao team:
“
traveled across the country to collect dialects, refining the granularity of accents down to the district and county level within cities…and they conducted a series of stylized dialogue training sessions on the model, giving it a distinct personality
.”
ByteDance also invested in its foundation model capabilities. In early February 2025, it hired Yonghui Wu, former Vice President of Google DeepMind, to manage theoretical research on large language models in its Seed team.
Tencent goes all-in to catchup and leapfrog ByteDance. In 2025, it poured more resources into its Yuanbao chatbot, which is based on its Hunyuan large model. The clearest evidence of this shift came in September 2025, when it poached 27-year-old Shunyu Yao, a former OpenAI researcher.
First, let’s back up. Since 2023, Tencent took a cautious approach to LLMs, neither actively stockpiling compute nor making huge talent acquisitions. Its Yuanbao launched a year later than Doubao, and even then, it was mostly positioned as “a product for Tencent to test its Hunyuan large language model technology.” An early Yuanbao team member told LatePost, “
Yuanbao was ‘in a state of begging other internal departments for collaboration
.’”
After Yao took charge of Tencent’s large model, he called an internal meeting in November 2025, where he showed up wearing shorts and slippers. Gao reports, ‘
There are big problems with Hunyuan's evaluation,’ a person present at the meeting quoted Yao  as saying, meaning that the model was overly focused on achieving high rankings on leaderboards, incorporating benchmark data into the training set, leading to data contamination. Although the model was good at answering questions, its performance in real-world scenarios was unstable. He hoped that, in the future, the team wouldn't focus on rankings or let the rankings dictate their work
.“
Some of the fixes were unsexy affairs, such as addressing data standardization issues. Here’s the relevant LatePost passage: “
A source close to Yuanbao told us that TEG’s data cleaning and labeling in small model training were not standardized. Taking the LBS (location-based services) intent recognition model as an example: a user asking ‘What are some good restaurants nearby? [周边有什么好吃的？]’ is a (prompt with) clear intent; while ‘What would you recommend that are good things to eat [有什么好吃的推荐]?’ is a (prompt with) vague intent. If these two types of samples were mixed and labeled together in the training set, the model would easily ‘learn incorrectly,’ leading to an increased error rate.”
In addition to improving its Hunyuan model, Yuanbao also integrated DeepSeek. Tencent finally put in a big order on computing cards to deploy DeepSeek across Tencent products, and Yuanbao climbed into the top three daily active users among Chinese AI apps. That came with trade-offs for Hunyuan, though: “
This meant the Yuanbao team couldn’t validate some of their ideas on the product, and many desired features couldn’t be implemented
.”
What are each giant’s respective superpowers? Let’s start with Alibaba, which positions itself as one of the few Chinese tech companies with “full-stack capabilities” that span from chips to large models to cloud infrastructure to end products and services.
The article compares Alibaba’s path to the one that Google has paved, “
According to our understanding, Alibaba’s PPU (Parallel Processing Unit) Zhenwu 810E has become one of the main chips in China’s newly added AI computing power market, finally securing its first external major customer order in 2025. On January 22, 2026, Alibaba decided to support its chip company, Pingtouge (T-Head), in a future independent listing.”
How do Tencent and ByteDance stack up? LatePost reports: “‘
At the end of the day, Tencent’s anxieties about traffic are much slighter than ByteDance,’ commented a ByteDance AI product manager. The latter seemingly has traffic engines like Douyin, Tomato Novel, and Toutiao, but the user profiles are very homogeneous, leaning towards entertainment, with limited scope for cross-business traffic redirection. Tencent, on the other hand, has leading products in almost every sector: WeChat, QQ, Tencent Video, Tencent Meeting, QQ Browser, QQ Music—they can find any type of user they want. This is their conviction.
”
And, some fun industry gossip: “This is more like an open intelligence war. A Yuanbao insider said, ‘
We discuss a feature point today, and Doubao knows about it the next day
.’ Doubao is also under pressure, ‘
They’ve poached almost all of our people.’
”
Let’s end with some notes on these giants’ structural advantages over the DeepSeeks and Zhipus of the world, in order of talent, compute, and data.
I found this peek into ByteDance’s recruitment practices to be fascinating: They scan all core majors from dozens of top Chinese universities and give their HR personnel the following target: “
the contact rate for undergraduate students in key majors at key universities should be above 80%, and the contact rate for master’s students should be above 90%
.” They’ve also sharpened their focus to recruit 10-20 of the most outstanding talents each year, with the standard defined as “
the potential to be among the top 40 at OpenAI
.”
With regards to compute, an AI founder shared a calculation exercise with the LatePost reporter: “
ByteDance, Alibaba, and Tencent each possess over 100,000 GPUs. For a startup, the monthly rental for an 8-card H100 server is approximately USD$10,000; using a conservative estimate of $10,000 per card per year, 100,000 cards would require an investment of approximately $1 billion per year—and this is just the minimum threshold to stay in the game when competing with the giants
.”
The giants also have a lot of internal data that other companies can’t access through the public internet.
I do want to emphasize one passage that reflects the Chinese giants’ disadvantages compared to the U.S. AI leaders. From the article: “
A source close to ByteDance’s senior management said that ByteDance’s current infrastructure is stronger than any other company in China. However, compared to the global landscape, the biggest problem is the lack of people like those at OpenAI who can propose directions and conduct cutting-edge research, such as GPT-4o and Sora. ‘China didn’t have true corporate research institutes in the past because private companies were too poor; now they can finally give it a try.’
” As the article states in another section, “
It is understood that ByteDance aims to become at least the third largest player globally in large language models
” — note that the goal is top 3, not number 1.

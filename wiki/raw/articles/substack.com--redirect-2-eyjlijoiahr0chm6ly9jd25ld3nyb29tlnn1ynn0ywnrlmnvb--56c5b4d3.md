# Exclusive | MediaTek Hires Intel’s Packaging Heavyweight. Can He Rescue EMIB-T?

Hello everyone,
In last week’s newsletter, I promised to analyze the earnings calls of major companies such as MediaTek（聯發科） and Delta Electronics（台達電）.
Unfortunately, I have to break that promise, because MediaTek made a major personnel announcement yesterday:
“We are pleased to announce that Mr. Navid Shahriari, who brings 37 years of experience in semiconductor design engineering and advanced packaging at Intel, along with substantial hands-on experience in the data center market, will join MediaTek as Senior Vice President on November 9.”
When I looked into his background, I was stunned: “MediaTek has actually brought in the top executive responsible for EMIB-T to fix EMIB-T?”
Shahriari is a heavyweight Intel veteran. He came up through design engineering (DEG) and sort and test technology development.
He was highly regarded by Pat Gelsinger, who announced in October 2024 that Shahriari would take charge of process technology development—a position comparable in stature to that of Y.J. Mii（米玉傑）, TSMC’s current co-chief operating officer.
But Gelsinger was abruptly ousted by Intel’s board. After Lip-Bu Tan（陳立武） took over, he broke up Intel’s sprawling foundry and manufacturing organization. Shahriari was promoted to executive vice president (EVP), taking charge of the newly independent back-end chip manufacturing division, overseeing advanced packaging and testing operations, including EMIB, EMIB-T, and Foveros.
Then, in June this year, Tan recruited former SK Hynix CEO Seok-Hee Lee as executive vice president to replace Shahriari and take over Intel’s advanced packaging business, sending shockwaves through the industry.
Shahriari announced his retirement at the same time.
MediaTek announced that Shahriari will “lead the advanced process and yield platform, design technology co-optimization, advanced testing and packaging, and quality and reliability teams.”
Longtime executive Chingsan Wu（吳慶杉）, who previously oversaw these operations, will now report to him. Shahriari, in turn, will report to President and Chief Operating Officer Joe Chen（陳冠州）.
Shahriari’s arrival brings my investigation over the past several days to a close.
The story begins with TSMC’s earnings call two weeks ago. Earlier reports suggested that if Intel’s EMIB-T packaging ran into difficulties, TSMC would be willing to take over packaging production for Google’s ninth-generation TPU, which uses the technology.
When an analyst asked about this, TSMC CEO C.C. Wei（魏哲家） responded with surprising candor. TSMC’s top priority, he said, was to ensure that its customers’ products could launch successfully. (Google is expected to become a major customer for TSMC’s 2nm and 3nm processes.)
“Is that hint clear enough?”
he said.
But doesn’t that amount to a tacit admission that
EMIB-T really is in trouble?
And wouldn’t that also spell trouble for MediaTek, which is collaborating with Google on the design of its ninth-generation TPU?
At MediaTek’s earnings call last Friday,
CEO Rick Tsai personally vouched for EMIB-T’s progress. He also made clear that the company would not change course: this “U.S. IDM packaging technology” remains MediaTek’s absolute top priority.
His confidence stems from MediaTek’s strong manufacturing team and its highly experienced fab operations executive. “Correction, I should say retired executive,” Tsai said. He was clearly referring to former TSMC Senior Vice President of Operations J.K. Lin（林錦坤）, who now serves as a part-time adviser.
But a source familiar with the situation told me that even MediaTek’s manufacturing veterans have been unable to make much headway,
because “they can’t get Intel’s people to move.”
It’s a classic case of one side scrambling while the other seems in no hurry at all.
According to sources, Intel engineers have even complained: “Why are you MediaTek people so aggressive about following up on issues? You ask a question yesterday and expect an answer today. Isn’t it normal to respond a week later?”
MediaTek therefore urgently needed a senior executive who could get Intel’s people moving.
Over the past several days, executives from different companies have told me that a senior TSMC executive with an Intel background was expected to jump ship to MediaTek. If the move went through, it would undoubtedly send shockwaves through both TSMC and MediaTek.
But I never imagined that
the person joining MediaTek would be someone even more senior: Shahriari himself.
Shahriari will be the highest-ranking non-ethnic-Chinese executive at a major Taiwanese technology company since Gianfranco Lanci’s tenure at Acer.
His arrival also adds another heavyweight to the growing ranks of Intel veterans among MediaTek’s senior executives involved in ASIC design services. They include Vince Hu, who has recently attracted considerable attention as Senior Vice President and General Manager of the Data Center and Computing Business Group, and T.Y. Lau（劉添益）, General Manager of Global Supply Chain Management, who came from Intel’s Arizona operations.
A senior MediaTek executive stressed that this was not a case of poaching.
MediaTek only approached Shahriari after he retired from Intel in June this year.
But competition for his services was fierce, including from major cloud providers that have begun developing their own AI chips.
“Everyone was competing for him. Quite a few companies approached him.”
That is because
“the entire world is looking for senior packaging talent,”
the executive said.
Packaging technology has become the single most important factor in AI chip competitiveness.
“In the past, packaging might have accounted for just 20% of a chip’s cost. Now it’s half,” he said.
Moreover, chipmakers have limited room to differentiate their products when using TSMC’s leading-edge front-end processes, while packaging technology offers countless possible combinations and configurations.
MediaTek executives are also pleased that Shahriari chose to join the company.
“He sees our momentum, too. If we had approached him three years ago, he might not have joined us.”
Going forward, Shahriari, who is of Indian descent, will spend half his working time in Taiwan.
Yet despite outsiders labeling Shahriari the “savior of EMIB-T,”
MediaTek has repeatedly emphasized that the arrival of this Intel heavyweight “is not about EMIB-T.”
Its official statement made no mention of Intel. Instead, it specifically mentioned TSMC
, stating that one of Shahriari’s core missions would be “to deepen collaboration with key supply chain partners, including TSMC, in line with MediaTek’s future development strategy, and to help MediaTek continue delivering highly competitive artificial intelligence and high-performance computing products through close collaboration and integration of advanced technologies.”
“We can’t afford to upset TSMC,” a former TSMC executive cautioned.

# Nvidia May Be Bankrolling Unimicron’s Expansion. Why Is Jensen Huang Nervous Despite the AI Boom?

Hello everyone,
First, a few words about yesterday’s TSMC earnings call.
The financial results were largely in line with expectations. What was interesting, however, was that C.C. Wei took the unusual step of giving substantive answers to questions about current developments, turning the earnings call into something of a press conference.
1. Wei acknowledged that TSMC is in discussions with Elon Musk’s Terafab project in Texas, “to see whether we can also utilize some of their additional capacity.”
But he emphasized that “the fabs must be operated by TSMC.” This suggests that
the chances of reaching an agreement with Musk are slim.
Share
2. Wei acknowledged that TSMC has begun considering building a fab in Texas
, and has already dispatched a team to evaluate the possibility.
However, a semiconductor industry veteran told me that although TSMC’s current capacity falls far short of demand, the company is simultaneously expanding more than a dozen fabs, leaving its management resources and workforce stretched thin. He believes it would be difficult for TSMC to open another front.
An analyst familiar with TSMC agreed, but she believes Texas could become an expansion option after 2030. By then, TSMC’s expansion in Arizona should be largely complete, allowing personnel to be reassigned.
3. Regarding the purchase of silicon interposers from GlobalFoundries, Wei said TSMC currently faces a substantial shortfall in back-end capacity. Since the company’s top priority is helping its “customers succeed in the marketplace,”
TSMC and GlobalFoundries will jointly support TSMC’s customers.
4. As for speculation that
TSMC would be willing to step in (for Google’s TPU) if Intel’s EMIB-T packaging runs into difficulties
, Wei started laughing before the question was even finished.
He said he did not really want to answer the question directly. But (as with the previous question), TSMC’s top priority is ensuring that its customers can successfully bring their products to market.
“That should be enough of a hint,” he said.
(In other words, yes, TSMC is willing!)
Now, back to this week’s newsletter.
A couple of days ago, a video was released showing Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang（黃仁勳） taking renowned technology podcaster Lex Fridman to Computex in Taipei and on a tour of a night market.
Between eating peanut ice cream rolls and drinking savory soy milk, Huang also discussed his approach to Taiwan’s supply chain: “Long-term relationships matter most,” and “It’s not just about choosing partners, but choosing people you want to work with for decades.”
I have also noticed what appears to be a major shift in Nvidia’s supply chain strategy this year. The company has been committing substantial funding to help substrate manufacturers and semiconductor assembly and testing companies expand capacity in Taiwan, Japan, and the United States.
What does this suggest about the industry’s outlook? Is there something Jensen Huang is worried about?

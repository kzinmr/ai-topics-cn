# American Data Center Hegemony - the Center-Satellite Structure

> There Is No A.I. “Race”: Chang Che debunks the “What About China” question
GPT: Che’s argument is that national advantage comes from adopting AI effectively, not reaching the technological frontier first.
1. Historical technology leaders often lost to countries better at widespread implementation.
2. Better models don’t automatically produce military dominance; deployment faces practical and institutional obstacles.
3. Fear of China helps companies justify deregulation and rushing development. Che cites slow military integration, bioweapon production difficulties, and diplomatic constraints on cyberattacks to challenge scenarios of sudden AI-enabled domination
4. Safety, public trust, and workforce training could strengthen America’s position.
5. Overall he disputes a winner-takes-all sprint.
I think a steelman of this would have to talk about distillation; the more American companies race, the faster Chinese companies can improve their models. Like rubber banding in video games, racing well brings your opponent closer to you, thus making it look like there's more of a race than there is.
But to say there is no race? Come on. Race dynamics already exist among Silicon Valley companies alone. 1386 employees of U.S. frontier labs signed the "Pacing the Frontier" letter[1], which talks about these race dynamics explicitly, but Sam Altman of OpenAI and Demis Hassabis of DeepMind notably did not sign. Of course, the letter argues for regulation and mutual agreements, not an unfettered race, and people in Silicon Valley consider it very important to include China in talks of a slowdown. I would also argue that Chinese companies tend to be quite competent and could properly catch up if America "paused"*, which is among the reasons why American companies don't want to pause.
* a full pause is not possible; read "pause" as "slowed down as much as conceivable"
One must also *have* a technology to leverage it. In case of conflict with China, one can cut off the other, which creates a reason to race in advance.
And is China not going for military dominance? They look like they have that goal to me, and since they are so skilled at manufacturing in civilian sectors, I think it's reasonable to infer they can do military too. Meanwhile Trump wants to increase the military budget to $1.5 trillion... The U.S. obviously can't afford that, and I think it's a good bet that Trump would not defend Taiwan just as he cut off all weapons and humanitarian packages to Ukraine, as if he just loves military spending only as an end in itself. But the Trump admin declared Anthropic a "supply chain risk" and blocked access to Fable 5 because Anthropic refused to give in to U.S. demands about killer robots and mass domestic surveillance, which is suggestive of their military ambitions. In any case, the foundation of an arms race exists, and raw intelligence can be a very powerful and quickly mobilized military capability.
Overall this article has "AI is a normal technology" vibes. I dispute this. AI is a normal technology, but AGI and the "quasi-AGI" we have now is not. I refer you to this music video[2] and this drier treatment[3]. The latter is labeled "22th century", but as someone who follows the developments of 2026 closely, I would be quite surprised if the dynamics of [3] aren't clearly visible before 2040. In the meantime, please understand that there's a reason why AI agents keep breaking out of different AI labs, all of which thought they had secure sandboxes.

# Follow-up: MediaTek’s CoWoS Heavyweight Hire Isn’t About EMIB-What Its Statement Reveals

Source: https://substack.com/app-link/post?publication_id=3423408&post_id=196371433&utm_source=post-email-title&utm_campaign=email-post-title&isFreemail=true&r=2flx6&token=eyJ1c2VyX2lkIjo0MDg3NDgyLCJwb3N0X2lkIjoxOTYzNzE0MzMsImlhdCI6MTc3Nzg2OTk5NiwiZXhwIjoxNzgwNDYxOTk2LCJpc3MiOiJwdWItMzQyMzQwOCIsInN1YiI6InBvc3QtcmVhY3Rpb24ifQ.Xts1YtiJbpUEaDnK_81tXcpYGTwZL46yUOkmRJ5FvFU

Last Friday’s newsletter broke the news that Douglas Yu（余振華）, a retired vice president at TSMC, had taken on an advisory role at MediaTek—sparking a wave of industry speculation.
Early Saturday morning, I received a message from MediaTek spokesperson and Co-COO David Ku（顧大為）, who sought to clarify that
Yu’s role is part-time and unrelated to Intel’s much-discussed EMIB advanced packaging technology
.
EMIB is expected to be deployed in MediaTek’s second-generation TPU developed in partnership with Google, replacing TSMC’s CoWoS. The shift has been widely interpreted as a key signal of Intel’s push to regain ground in advanced packaging.
While
the original newsletter
did not explicitly state that Yu would be involved in EMIB, it did devote significant space to MediaTek’s challenges in scaling the technology for mass production.
As a result, “news of a master of advanced packaging like Douglas Yu joining naturally energized engineers within MediaTek who were familiar with the situation.”
However, several follow-up reports from online media were more direct. “Some reports claimed we brought Doug (Douglas Yu) in specifically to support EMIB, which led to a surge of inquiries directed at him—and created unnecessary complications for both us and TSMC,” Ku said.
The scale of the reaction came as something of a surprise. It suggests that even a retired TSMC executive can quickly be cast as a potential “defector” if perceived to be assisting a major customer—especially one like Google or MediaTek, both of which remain key clients of TSMC’s 3nm and 2nm process technologies as well as CoWoS—on projects involving a rival’s technology. (It also underscores just how sensitive the EMIB issue has become.)
Ironically, Yu himself was portrayed in a newsletter I wrote a year ago—titled
“
Darth Vader in Semiconductor Industry: How the Turncoat and the Loyalist of TSMC Shaped the Global Chip War
”
—as a symbol of loyalty to TSMC.
MediaTek’s official statement reads:
“Following Mr. Douglas Yu’s retirement from TSMC, we are honored to invite him to serve as a part-time advisor to MediaTek. We look forward to leveraging his extensive industry experience and technical expertise to support the company’s forward-looking exploration and roadmap planning in advanced packaging technologies, while also guiding our continued investment and R&D efforts in TSMC-based advanced packaging solutions.”
Ku emphasized three key points in the statement:
1.
Yu serves as a part-time advisor and is not based in Taiwan,
limiting the depth of his day-to-day involvement.
2.
Yu’s work centers on pathfinding and exploration—terminology that, in TSMC’s R&D context, typically refers to technologies that are still three to ten years away from mass production. In other words,
it has no direct connection to EMIB
, which is already entering production.
3.
Rather than supporting EMIB, Yu’s role is expected to strengthen MediaTek’s collaboration with TSMC in advanced packaging—
effectively ensuring that the recent EMIB-related developments do not set a precedent going forward
.

# Why Is Nvidia Doubling Down on Taiwanese Suppliers After Jensen Huang’s Exit?

Source: https://substack.com/app-link/post?publication_id=3423408&post_id=225773479&utm_source=substack&utm_medium=email&utm_content=share&utm_campaign=email-share&action=share&triggerShare=true&isFreemail=true&r=2flx6&token=eyJ1c2VyX2lkIjo0MDg3NDgyLCJwb3N0X2lkIjoyMjU3NzM0NzksImlhdCI6MTc5NTE3MTA5MywiZXhwIjoxNzk3NzYzMDkzLCJpc3MiOiJwdWItMzQyMzQwOCIsInN1YiI6InBvc3QtcmVhY3Rpb24ifQ.sz0_W1rRNrz0K1XY8AYrSe4mStVwtVJBo0W-43dr41g

Hello everyone,
More than 20 years ago, when I first started covering the mobile phone industry, I witnessed a scene that left me stunned.
Motorola, then the world’s largest mobile phone maker, was holding a supplier conference in Taiwan. Before the evening banquet began, a group of Taiwanese supplier executives were actually instructed to line up on both sides of the red carpet to welcome the arrival of Motorola’s American executives. Among them were Cheng Uei Precision Industry（正崴） Chairman T.C. Gou（郭台強） and Tatung（大同） Chairman Wei-shan Lin（林蔚山）, both standing ramrod straight like guards awaiting their superiors.
That absurd scene perfectly illustrated
the condescending attitude of major American corporations toward their suppliers at the time: “We are superior, and you are subordinate.”
Even after the iPhone era began, little changed under either Steve Jobs or Tim Cook.
It was not until the AI boom that Taiwanese suppliers suddenly found themselves upgraded from economy class to first class.

# Inside the AI Rack War: MediaTek and Foxconn Race Toward the Copper-to-Optics Era

Hello everyone,
Even though superstar Jensen Huang（黃仁勳） dominated most of the spotlight, I believe the real highlight of this year’s Computex 2026 was the keynote delivered by Matt Murphy, chairman and CEO of Marvell Technology.
I’ve followed Marvell for years. In China, the company is known as “Meiman Electronics,” and for a long time its reputation was overshadowed by its infamous founding team. Add to that the fact that Marvell previously lost a major AWS AI ASIC order to Alchip Technologies（世芯）, and I had consistently underestimated the company.
But after hearing Murphy precisely describe Marvell’s decade-long transformation, I finally understood why this liberal arts graduate from Franklin & Marshall College — someone without an especially glamorous résumé — had once been a serious CEO candidate targeted by Intel’s board. Only after Murphy declined did Lip-Bu Tan（陳立武） eventually take the role.
In 2016, Marvell’s founding team exited following an accounting scandal. Murphy was brought in as an emergency replacement and immediately launched a sweeping restructuring effort, aiming to transform what had been a second-tier chip designer heavily dependent on consumer markets into a world-class provider of data infrastructure semiconductor solutions.
Keep in mind: that was the same year AlphaGo defeated Lee Sedol（李世乭）. Very few people at the time understood that AI would eventually reshape the world. Murphy’s foresight was remarkable.
He made several critical decisions.
He exited the smartphone market and sold off Marvell’s Wi-Fi and Bluetooth businesses.
Through a highly targeted series of acquisitions, he assembled the key technology building blocks required for cloud data centers and AI servers — including the acquisition of Avera Semiconductor, formerly IBM’s ASIC design division. More importantly, Marvell acquired high-speed optical interconnect and signal processing company Inphi for $10 billion in 2021, instantly turning Marvell into a leader in silicon photonics.
Murphy believed that staying competitive required access to the most advanced manufacturing nodes available. So Marvell jumped directly from 14/16nm, skipped 7nm entirely, and moved aggressively into 5nm — a strategy that ultimately proved highly successful.
Of course, one key reason Marvell’s market capitalization now exceeds MediaTek’s by a noticeable margin was Nvidia’s $2 billion investment in March.
Why did Murphy earn Jensen Huang’s support?
My view is that Nvidia wants to pull this “mini-Broadcom” — a company that combines networking and ASIC capabilities — into its ecosystem and help strengthen it as a counterweight to Broadcom, which is increasingly emerging as Nvidia’s biggest rival in AI infrastructure.

I believe Murphy’s keynote also set the tone for this year’s Computex.
He argued that AI infrastructure performance depends on three key pillars: compute, memory, and connectivity.
Previously, compute and memory were the bottlenecks. But going forward, connectivity will become the limiting factor because the industry is approaching the “copper wall”
— the physical limits of transmitting data through copper cables and copper traces.
Naturally, Murphy has every incentive to promote his own products. But he is fundamentally correct.
Over the past two years, GPUs were the primary constraint.
Today, memory is the bottleneck. Next, as the industry enters the “copper-to-optics” transition, this entirely new technological shift could trigger a period of major disruption.
And we can already glimpse this brewing “war inside the rack” through two seemingly insignificant metal connectors displayed at the booths of MediaTek and Foxconn.

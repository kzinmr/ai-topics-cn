Hello everyone,
During TSMC’s first-quarter earnings call in mid-April, there was a striking moment.
After TSMC posted a record-breaking gross margin of 66.2%, an analyst asked CEO C.C. Wei（魏哲家）whether, given that some players in the AI supply chain are reporting even stronger profitability, the company should consider raising prices.
The reference, of course, was to South Korea’s two memory giants. Amid a severe memory shortage, SK Hynix delivered a first-quarter operating margin of 72%, far exceeding TSMC’s 58.1%.
Meanwhile, Samsung Electronics reported first-quarter operating profit of $38.6 billion—nearly double TSMC’s $20.8 billion.
With this memory supercycle expected to last for years, a question arises: could Samsung, long pressured by TSMC in the foundry space, leverage memory’s scarcity to push customers toward its foundry services and narrow the gap?
The first signs of “memory-for-foundry” bundling have already emerged. According to an analyst, Apple recently sought to procure a large volume of DRAM from Samsung.
Samsung’s terms: a 100% price increase—along with a condition that Apple shift orders for smartphone camera image sensors (CIS) from Sony to Samsung.
This business is not only Sony’s cash cow, but also a key product for JASM’s Kumamoto fab—a joint venture between TSMC and Sony—manufactured on TSMC’s 28/22nm process.
Rumor has it that Apple agreed without hesitation.
Will this bundling strategy extend to smartphone SoCs—or even AI chips, which are far more memory-intensive?
It is a development worth watching.

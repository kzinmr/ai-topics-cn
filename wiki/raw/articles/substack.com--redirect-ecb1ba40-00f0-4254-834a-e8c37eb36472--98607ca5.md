# The CoPoS Moment: As Google Eyes Intel, Can TSMC Reinforce Its Competitive Moat?

Source: https://substack.com/redirect/ecb1ba40-00f0-4254-834a-e8c37eb36472?j=eyJ1IjoiMmZseDYifQ.6O_iC1_GWCzChnbrHzSrs1hMpJpoODTUJm_FAk3tf6E

Hello everyone,
“How about that?”
That was the closing line from C.C. Wei (魏哲家) during TSMC’s earnings call on the 16th.
Unfortunately, the call was held virtually, so we couldn’t see his expression—but it wasn’t hard to imagine the intensity behind it.
The final question from analysts centered on one of the biggest highlights from Nvidia’s GTC this year: a newly unveiled high-speed, inference-focused LPU.
Because the chip is manufactured by Samsung, it has been widely viewed as a major breakthrough for one of TSMC’s key rivals in the AI race.
A TSMC IR executive quickly issued a stern reminder: the question touched on specific customers and products, and under company policy, such topics should not be addressed.
Wei acknowledged the warning—but then brushed it aside.
“We’ve already started working with the customer on a second LPU,” he said, before adding: “We will capture every piece of business we can.”
It was, in effect, a message directed at Samsung: TSMC has already taken the business back—and intends to leave nothing on the table. He then closed with that slightly provocative line: “How about that?”
The tone of this exchange, in my view, marked a clear departure from past earnings calls.
Following the AI boom, both Samsung and Intel had suffered a string of setbacks, falling further behind TSMC.
At one point, there were even concerns that TSMC’s surging market share could attract antitrust scrutiny from regulators worldwide.
But the landscape has shifted dramatically this year.
A memory supercycle has driven a sharp surge in Samsung’s profits—allowing it to surpass TSMC this quarter, and potentially even overtake Nvidia next year to become the world’s most profitable company.
Intel, meanwhile, is staging a comeback, fueled by a resurgence in CPU demand tied to agentic AI. Its stock has risen about 40% over the past month.
And then there’s Elon Musk, the world’s richest man, who is reportedly aligning with Intel and Samsung to build a proprietary “TeraFab”—an indirect challenge to TSMC’s dominance.
Against this backdrop, despite once again delivering results that beat expectations, TSMC found itself fielding more questions about competition than performance. At one point, Wei appeared visibly irritated—shifting into a take-no-prisoners stance.

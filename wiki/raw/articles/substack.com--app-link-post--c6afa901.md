Hello everyone,
With memory shortages becoming the biggest bottleneck facing AI data centers today, soaring memory prices have recently triggered a chain reaction across the industry.
Micron’s gross margin has surged to an astonishing 84.6%, with some brokerages even forecasting it could reach 90% next year.
That reminded me of something Phison（群聯）CEO K.S. Pua（潘健成）said during an exclusive subscriber event for this newsletter. He remarked that an 80% gross margin for memory was enough and that companies should not be too greedy. “If it climbs to 90%, you’ll get hit with antitrust lawsuits,” he said.
As it turned out, all three major DRAM makers are now facing antitrust litigation in the United States, where consumers have filed a class-action lawsuit accusing them of price manipulation.
Almost simultaneously came the industry’s stunning public exchange between Apple and Micron.
Apple announced sweeping price increases. While iPhone prices have yet to be adjusted, virtually all of its other products will see increases ranging from 10% to 30%.
In an interview with
The Wall Street Journal
, Tim Cook argued that the sharp increases in memory prices were ultimately hurting consumers.
A few days later, the same reporter interviewed Micron’s Chief Business Officer. He fired back, saying today’s severe memory supply shortage exists because “certain customers” took advantage of the industry during its downturn.
“Because prices were terrible and gross margins were terrible, investment across the entire industry was effectively forced to stop in 2023,” he said.
What I found even more interesting, however, was that while Micron dominated the headlines, Qualcomm CEO Cristiano Amon appeared on Bloomberg Television to discuss Qualcomm’s expansion into AI data centers.
He was remarkably unfazed by the current memory shortage, saying
it has no impact on Qualcomm because its AI ASIC solution relies on the company’s innovative HBC (High Bandwidth Compute) technology, allowing it to avoid relying on expensive standard HBM
.
He also said the business is expected to generate US$15 billion in AI data center revenue by fiscal 2029. (Macquarie’s latest estimate projects that MediaTek’s ASIC business will contribute US$40 billion in revenue by 2028.)
In reality,
Qualcomm is using customized HBM developed jointly with Nanya Technology（南亞科）(and possibly other partners)
. Nanya President Pei-ing Lee（李培瑛）has long kept the identity of the key customer behind this strategic business confidential. The answer has now been revealed.
On Qualcomm’s supplier page for its Dragonfly data center platform appears a statement from Lee:
“...This represents an important milestone in our collaboration with Qualcomm. Nanya is honored to provide innovative and reliable technologies that support the growing demands of AI and next-generation data centers.”
Back to this week’s newsletter.
Taiwan’s technology stocks are currently in a period of extraordinary speculation.
Several of my interviewees, all of whom occupy key positions within TSMC’s CoPoS supply chain, have become so frustrated by the flood of inaccurate rumors surrounding CoPoS that they say their “blood pressure shoots up” whenever they see them, and some admit they are “furious every time.”
I therefore began by conducting in-depth interviews with them about what I call the “glass myth” surrounding CoPoS.

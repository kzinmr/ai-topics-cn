Traditional, full-length palace dramas indeed look very different. Female leads typically conform to the mainstream beauty standard of pale, young and thin. The endings generally fall into the following categories: The male or female lead dies tragically; “I won the throne, but lost everything I loved”; “I won the throne, but became like the people I hated”; both leads retire to live a quiet life away from court; the female lead forgives the emperor his various sins and they live happily ever after at court.
“Don’t Mess with a Chubby Empress”looked like a palace drama but turned the genre on its head, injecting it with values that felt more modern. Its empress was feisty, focused on making money and completely uninterested in the emperor. Rather than joining the concubines in fighting for the emperor’s favor, she gave him the cold shoulder.
People can watch short dramas on their smartphones in the tiniest increments of time at their disposal. They don’t need to wait for new episodes like in some TV dramas. Because the stories are designed around the possibility that you might swipe away at any second, they offer viewers a heightened, condensed experience and a seemingly infinite library of possibility and entertainment. But what appears to be a world of choice is in fact a handful of tried and tested tropes. A short drama is a deconstructed story, rebuilt from what triggers the most emotional response.

Short dramas draw from an existing bank of stories produced by the web novel industry: “Smartphone users in China do one of three things,” a feature film and short drama director Holmes Deng told me: “Play games, watch short videos, or read web novels.”
Short dramas are about big emotions and dramatic reversals; the heroes and villains are generally clearly delineated. They rely on formulas of hook (often within the first three seconds), plot twist and cliffhanger.

They operated on a serialization model, where a reader could expect several new chapters each day. Short dramas tapped into these stories of time travel, revenge, and high fantasy — and translated the narrative patterns of web novels into video.
For romance-oriented short dramas, common plot lines include forced marriages, CEOs falling for ordinary women and childhood lovers being reunited. Revenge dramas often start with the death of the lead, who is then reborn, ready to exact their revenge. If a main character is poor or downtrodden, they either ascend to the top echelons of society or reveal that they were secretly rich to begin with. I spoke to Caroline Chen, who runs a styling company catering to both long and short dramas, about the popularity of the genre. The last short drama she styled was a contemporary family drama where the main character was a baby with magic powers.
Short dramas first became popular in China’s mid-sized cities, she explained. The leads were often a cleaning lady or a security guard. These dramas were “a lower-tier-market phenomenon”; underdog stories consumed by underdogs. Their audiences didn’t care that the costumes weren’t high-quality, that the actors were often inexperienced, that there were no expensive special effects, or any semblance of realism.
In online discussions, web users argued that short dramas gave them more choice than traditional ones. They wanted to see new faces. They wanted to be carried on the emotional arc of a story in the same time it took for them to finish their commute. One-minute episodes were a better fit for fragmented and exhausting work schedules.
“It gives people what they want,” said a friend, a recent convert to short dramas. “Instead of watching 88 episodes [of a traditional TV drama] and only feeling satisfied at the end.” She also recognized their addictive quality; she has downloaded then deleted the short-drama app Fanqie seven times, she told me.
After capturing the mass market, short dramas spread to China’s biggest cities and its white-collar workers. Over time, lead actors became more selective about the scripts and productions they chose. While older short dramas typically went straight into intense conflict, newer productions develop the relationship between the characters more gradually and with more nuance.
One of last year’s hits
, “Midsummer Fendra,” started with a forced engagement, but the leads didn’t have inexplicable, intense chemistry: Instead, the relationship was built through small gestures, misunderstandings and reconciliations.The acting was more subtle, less slapstick. The audience wields a great deal of influence on the genre. Directors receive immediate feedback on their drama from streaming platforms, which track when viewers skip ahead or stop watching using analytics, Deng explained. They can see what works and what doesn’t, and exactly where the audience loses interest and drops off.
All these dramas come with comment sections, similar to social media posts. People often watch them with comments from other viewers drifting across their screens. These dramas are designed to have viewers rooting for the main characters. Tencent Video, a popular platform to watch longform dramas, introduced a function where you can
set the speed of play
according to which character is on screen (1.5x or 2x for the ones you don’t like). Short dramas are already designed to give the leads more screen time.
Their focus is on crowd-pleasing scenes like romantic escalation. One friend commented that watching romantic short dramas sometimes felt like watching a string of kiss scenes.

On the Hongguo app, the most popular short drama app, owned by ByteDance, dramas are sorted into six settings — the countryside, the office, the campus, ancient-historical, Republican era and period dramas — and 34 plot themes, which include revenge takedowns, high fantasy where characters cultivate spiritual energy, miracle-working doctor, mystery, secret identity and elite family feuds.
Users can also pick from 17 main characters, including strong female lead, cute kid, underdog, ultra-rich tycoon, switched-at-birth heiress, female emperor, doppelgänger love interest, enemies-to-lovers. There’s something for everyone, and as much of it as you could wish for.

He explained that one of the reasons most short drama consumers are women is that men want more action scenes and explosions, which are hard to execute on short drama budgets. AI could make those possible for a fraction of the cost.

Hello everyone,
It’s been a packed week for tech news. Google just unveiled its eighth-generation TPU, and TSMC held its North America Technology Symposium—both developments with significant implications for Taiwan’s supply chain. I’ll cover those in next week’s newsletter.
The most consequential development, however, is Apple’s sudden leadership transition.
At 50, Senior Vice President of Hardware Engineering John Ternus will take over as CEO on September 1. Tim Cook will move into the role of Executive Chairman.
That title is worth unpacking.
The first time I encountered “Executive Chairman” was in 2013, when Paul Jacobs handed over the CEO role at Qualcomm to Steve Mollenkopf, while retaining influence under that same designation.
Executives later told me the transition was anything but smooth. Mollenkopf had informed Jacobs that Microsoft was courting him for a CEO role (likely as one of several candidates). To retain a key lieutenant, Jacobs stepped aside—but not entirely.
The “executive” qualifier allowed him to stay involved in operations. Under U.S. corporate governance norms, a non-executive chairman’s authority is typically confined to the board.
I saw the term again last March, when Micron Technology announced the appointment of Mark Liu as a board member, describing his prior role at TSMC (2018–2024) as “executive chairman.”
That raised some questions. In Taiwan, Liu’s title was simply “chairman.” But the “executive” qualifier makes sense in a Western context: under Morris Chang’s design, Liu remained actively involved in key operational decisions—unlike the typically hands-off chairman role in the U.S.
TSMC’s PR team confirmed as much: in practice, Taiwan’s “chairman” often aligns more closely with the Western notion of an “executive chairman”—what Chang once described as a “chairman who runs the business.”
Seen in this light, Apple’s succession story may still have more chapters ahead.
Turning to this week’s focus.
Amid the recent surge in Taiwan equities, a second NT$10,000 stock has emerged: WinWay Technology（穎崴）.
A few years ago, when we were working on a semiconductor cover story in Kaohsiung, a colleague interviewed this “hidden champion” in IC test interfaces. At the time, it was a relatively unremarkable, blue-collar operation. Few would have predicted today’s breakout.
Fewer still would have expected its deep ties with Nvidia.
But does its industry position truly justify its meteoric valuation?

# Trump warns against losing AI race to China despite safety concerns

Source: https://substack.com/redirect/a346bc30-136e-406b-902e-0e9d10a47d40?j=eyJ1IjoiMmZseDYifQ.6O_iC1_GWCzChnbrHzSrs1hMpJpoODTUJm_FAk3tf6E

President Trump on Sunday
downplayed the growing concerns
over artificial intelligence’s rapid development and its implications for the future of humanity, saying guardrails can be placed on the technology but that the U.S. cannot lose the AI race to China in the process.
“Well, I say this, we’re leading China on AI. We’re the most sophisticated country in the world and frankly I want to keep it that way, because whoever wins AI, wins,” Trump told reporters
at his golf club
in Doonbeg, Ireland, according to a White House pool report.
“And we can put guardrails, and we can do this and that, but I think you have a lot of negative forces that are bringing it up and they’re bringing up things that won’t happen,” he added.
The president’s comments come days after a former Anthropic and OpenAI employee, Jacob Coxon, claimed in a post online that researchers at the firms “believe AI could kill all humans” but still push forward in a race to develop superintelligence — a hypothetical AI agent that outperforms all of humanity’s cognitive performance.
Coxon’s assessment went viral and prompted a chorus of officials, lawmakers and even some celebrities to
sound the alarm
on the potential dangers of AI.
Also on Sunday, Speaker Mike Johnson (R-La.) and House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries (D-N.Y.) weighed in on the topic, with the latter pushing for Congress to act urgently on AI safety. Johnson, echoing Trump, urged caution.
“If Congress just races in and does some sort of emergency session to try to regulate AI, we will lose the race to China, and that is a threat to every single American. So, we’ve got to have balance. We’ve got to have steady hands at the wheel,” Johnson told CNN’s Jake Tapper on “State of the Union.”
“But we don’t need everybody to panic right now,” he added later. “We need to handle this new technology like we have others in the past, and make sure we’re doing everything we can, responsibly, to also not smother American innovation. We have to do both things simultaneously.”
Trump recently has railed against critics of data centers, the AI infrastructure that has become increasingly unpopular among Americans. In the Oval Office last week, he
suggested that data center opponents
want to “go through poverty, crime and squalor.”

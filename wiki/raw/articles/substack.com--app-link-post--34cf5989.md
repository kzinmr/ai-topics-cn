# Chris Miller：The AI Supply Chain, Huawei's "Tao Law," and the Next Wave of the China Model

Source: https://substack.com/app-link/post?publication_id=3423408&post_id=201709650&utm_source=podcast-email&play_audio=true&r=2flx6&utm_campaign=email-play-on-substack&token=eyJ1c2VyX2lkIjo0MDg3NDgyLCJwb3N0X2lkIjoyMDE3MDk2NTAsImlhdCI6MTc4MTI3MDA2NCwiZXhwIjoxNzgzODYyMDY0LCJpc3MiOiJwdWItMzQyMzQwOCIsInN1YiI6InBvc3QtcmVhY3Rpb24ifQ.xFKZ_EAPQzs3OBGf1i6z-5deBSzazUvYtf8gYB16SPs&utm_source=substack&utm_medium=email&utm_content=play_card#play

Two weeks ago, Chamath Palihapitiya—Silicon Valley VC and early Groq investor—dropped a bombshell on the All-In Podcast: Taiwan will lose its semiconductor strategic dominance within 18 months because the US is “only a nanometer or two away” from domestic self-sufficiency.
This sparked intense debate across Silicon Valley and the global tech community. We invited Chris Miller, author of
Chip War
and professor at Tufts University, back to decode this from a US perspective.
Miller’s response was blunt: “Nonsense. I don’t think that’s accurate.” TSMC is scaling up both in Arizona and aggressively in Taiwan. Even if Intel and Samsung capture 5% to 10% of the market, TSMC will remain far ahead. He emphasized that the wave of AI servers and advanced packaging has upgraded Taiwan from a component supplier to a critical R&D partner—the structural reason behind the rise of its broader tech ecosystem.
Regarding Huawei’s “Tau (τ) Law,” which claims to replace Moore’s Law, Miller noted it is essentially advanced packaging and 3D stacking that TSMC was doing a decade ago: “Less of a breakthrough, more of a PR stunt.” This episode also dives into the logic behind the US-China truce, the battle of scale vs. profit, and the emerging supply chain war over drones and physical AI.
【Key Takeaways From This Episode】
04:30 |
Taiwan losing its strategic position in 18 months? Miller: “Nonsense”
US self-sufficiency is a long way off. With TSMC holding nearly 100% market share, it will remain dominant for a long time, despite increased competition.
11:00 |
From TSMC to the broader ecosystem: Why Taiwan replaced Japan and Korea at the core of the AI supply chain
AI servers are growing more complex. Assembly know-how and gross margins are rising annually, while the advanced packaging ecosystem pushes more Taiwanese companies to the R&D frontlines.
13:00 |
The truth about Huawei’s “Tau (τ) Law”: Less of a breakthrough, more of a PR stunt
The slowing of Moore’s Law has been common knowledge for a decade. Huawei claims its “Logic Folding” will reach a 1.4nm equivalent by 2031, but Miller believes this actually signals their inability to catch up in traditional fabrication, aiming the narrative at Beijing and the domestic market.
18:00 |
The US-China truce and “Scale vs. Profit”: Both sides think time is on their side
China commands 40% of manufacturing capacity, but most of its industries operate on razor-thin margins; CATL (batteries) is a rare exception with a moat. Meanwhile, the US is betting on rare earth alternatives and its AI lead.
31:00 |
Drones and Physical AI: The critical nodes of the next supply chain war
Taiwan excels in electronics manufacturing, but its batteries and motor magnets (rare earths) remain bottlenecks tied to China. We must prevent the EV industry’s CATL dilemma from repeating in the robotics era.

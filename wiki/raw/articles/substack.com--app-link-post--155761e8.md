Before we get into this issue, two major developments deserve attention.
The first is, of course, a historic event: SpaceX’s IPO. What Taiwan’s technology industry cares about most is whether Elon Musk’s plan to launch more than one million Orbital Data Centers is actually progressing. After all, it could trigger the next major battle among companies such as Foxconn, Quanta, and Wistron for large-scale orders.
The latest update is that a batch of satellites known as “AI1” is scheduled to be launched by the end of next year for initial testing.
SpaceX’s prospectus explicitly states that these data centers are “not expected to undergo processor maintenance or repair in space,” but will instead rely on “extensive pre-launch testing.”
As a result, the ability to design, manufacture, and validate ultra-high-reliability orbital data centers is likely to become a new competitive battleground for Taiwan’s industry as opportunities emerge around space-based AI.
The second development may have escaped some people’s attention. On Tuesday, Jensen Huang flew to Texas and joined Coherent CEO Jim Anderson in breaking ground on the world’s first 6-inch indium phosphide (InP) wafer production line.
Episil-Precision（嘉晶） Chairman Jian-Hua Syu（徐建華） told me that Nvidia’s US$2 billion investment in Coherent three months ago was primarily intended to support this facility. Indium phosphide, which is used to manufacture the optical transmitters and receivers required for optical communications, has become a major bottleneck for co-packaged optics (CPO). “There simply isn’t enough supply today.”
The situation is further complicated by the fact that China controls roughly 60% of global indium phosphide substrate production capacity. In February 2025, Beijing imposed export controls on indium phosphide, effectively putting a chokehold on the CPO supply chain.
Only after Coherent’s CEO traveled to Beijing alongside Donald Trump for negotiations did China recently approve a new batch of export licenses for indium phosphide substrates.
For that reason, he believes the market is overly optimistic about the pace of CPO adoption. In his view, Jensen Huang’s repeated delays to the timeline for large-scale CPO deployment are undoubtedly linked to the tightening supply of indium phosphide.
Turning to this issue of the newsletter, MediaTek’s recent AI-driven transformation has won praise from foreign investors, triggering a dramatic re-rating that pushed the stock to consecutive limit-up gains and captured widespread attention.
But in reality, another company experienced a re-rating even earlier, delivering a surge that left many investors puzzled. That company was Delta Electronics, Taiwan’s third-largest technology company.
Over the past year, the veteran power-supply maker’s share price has risen 4.4 times, far outpacing MediaTek’s 2.5-fold gain.
